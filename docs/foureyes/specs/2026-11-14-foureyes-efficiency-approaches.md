# foureyes efficiency — approaches

BRIEF (verbatim): Investigate and propose efficiency tweaks to the foureyes plugin itself (this repo, lazypowers), including which model + effort combinations to use per task/role (drafter, approach proposers, spec critic, code critic, review/refute critics, investigators, scouts, implementers by modelTier, task reviewer). Goal: same or better finding quality at lower subscription usage and wall-clock. Evidence already gathered this session: critique-log ledger (91 logs) — 6.1-sol high: 280 rounds, 3.0 findings/rnd, 91% acted on, median 251s/round; Astra high (Sep 6–23): 53 rounds, 5.8/rnd, 94%, 210s; spec seam ~tie, code seam Astra 2.5 vs Sol 1.5 findings/rnd (n=13 vs 105). Codex session token data: 6.1-sol high median ~734k input (mostly cached), ~4k output, 6.9 credits/run (mean 8.5), ~1,682 runs since Sep 30 (~260/week); Astra high median 18.2 credits/run (mean 36.7). Astra credit rates 5x Sol. 6.1 Astra not released. xhigh on 6.1 Sol: ~+29% cost, +42% time, ~+1 intelligence point (Artificial Analysis). Approach seam yields 0.1 findings/rnd over 31 rounds. Codex review seam 0.7/rnd over 14 rounds. User is on ChatGPT Pro Lite (subscription, not API). Constraints: critic persona name stays "Sol" permanently; no CODEX_CRITIC_SPEED / fast tier.

## Proposed

### 1 — Cut Sol calls, hold the operating point
WHAT: Keep every model and effort pin except one, and remove calls the ledger says do not pay.
(1) Adaptive round budget for the spec and code seams: the gate block in scripts/codex-critic.sh (lines 141-152) returns `GATE: final` at round 1 when that round raised no Critical and at most 2 Important, so round 2 and its revision dispatch run only after a heavy round 1. The pipeline already accepts an unverified last revision after `final`; this moves it earlier on light rounds.
(2) Skip Sol's approach proposal when the brief names the approach. skills/foureyes-brainstorm/SKILL.md lines 251-269 currently force it "always"; Fable's A0 still does the feasibility check. Sol keeps proposing on open briefs.
(3) Route `mechanical` tasks to `haiku` on the existing `foureyes-implementer` agent. Its pinned `effort: medium` stays, so this is one row in the table at skills/foureyes-build/SKILL.md lines 201-205 and no new agent file.
(4) Matching edits to README.md (cost paragraph at lines 189-192, config table at 245-252) and to the tests under tests/ that assert the gate and the tier table.
Resulting role table: drafter fable/high (unchanged); Sol approach proposer gpt-6.1-sol/high, open briefs only; spec and code critic gpt-6.1-sol/high, round 2 conditional; review, refute and investigate on Codex gpt-6.1-sol/high (unchanged); Claude review and refute critics opus/high (unchanged); investigator high (unchanged); scouts sonnet/medium (unchanged); implementers mechanical haiku/medium, standard sonnet/medium, frontier opus/high; task reviewer sonnet/medium (unchanged).
Astra and xhigh are rejected on the brief's own numbers. Astra ties at the spec seam at 5x the rate. At the code seam it costs about 7.3 credits per finding against Sol's 4.6 (median run cost divided by findings per round), on n=13 from different weeks. xhigh costs +29% credits and +42% time for about one point.
COST: No new dependencies and no new files. Touches codex-critic.sh, two SKILL.md files, README.md and the tests. A feature's Sol calls drop from up to 5 to as few as 2, and each skipped round 2 also saves about 4 minutes plus one Fable or implementer revision cycle. The Important threshold of 2 is a pick, not a measured value; the spec must confirm it from the ledger's per-round split.
GIVES UP: The second cold read after light first rounds, so a regression introduced by a small revision goes unseen by Sol. Named-approach briefs lose the second family's menu. Haiku on mechanical tasks may bounce more often at the task reviewer; the existing "re-dispatch one tier up" rule (build SKILL.md line 240) is the only backstop. It does not answer whether gpt-6.1-terra or Sol at medium would do.
CHECKED: Claude Code subagent frontmatter accepts `model: haiku` and `effort: low|medium|high|xhigh|max` (search summary of code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents and /model-config; pages not opened). `-c model_reasoning_effort=` works per call (codex-critic.sh line 99). Not checked: the per-round findings split, the share of briefs that name an approach, and the share of tasks tiered mechanical.

### 2 — Keep strong discovery; amortize verification
WHAT: Retain the pipeline and its two-round limits. Use this operating baseline: drafter and Claude approach proposer Fable/high; Sol approach proposer, spec critic, code critic, reviewer and investigator gpt-6.1-sol/high; Claude reviewer/refuter Opus/high; Claude investigator explicitly Opus/high instead of inheriting the session; scouts, mechanical/standard implementers and task reviewer Sonnet/medium; frontier implementers Opus/high. Keep the persona "Sol" permanently. Reserve Astra/high for explicitly difficult code or investigations, with no automatic extra pass and no fast tier. Trial Sol/medium for approach proposals and refutations, and Sonnet/medium for Claude refutations, against the baseline before promotion. Batch related review refutations by receiving model and code area, preserving a separate decision for every finding: today skills/foureyes-review/SKILL.md starts a fresh call per disputed finding, while skills/foureyes-review/lib/review-synth.mjs already consumes decisions keyed by finding ID. Extend existing accounting to distinguish role, effective model/effort, round, cached input, output/reasoning, duration and retries. Compare settings on identical pre-fix snapshots, judging confirmed Important/Critical misses and false dismissals; judge approach proposers by useful contributions to the chosen direction. Touches scripts/codex-critic.sh, scripts/sol-ledger.mjs, scripts/pipeline-stats.mjs, their existing tests, agents/foureyes-investigator.md, agents/foureyes-refute-critic.md, the brainstorm/review/investigate/ledger skills, tests/codex-critic.test.sh, tests/prose-contracts.test.sh, and README.md.
COST: Approximately 15 existing files; no dependencies or consumer-data migration. Preserve old ledger parsing. Paired comparisons temporarily consume extra allowance. Batching adds response bookkeeping and can increase latency for an individual finding, so measure total review time as well as usage. Model/effort selection stays in the existing wrapper and agent definitions.
GIVES UP: Immediate blanket downgrades and promised percentage savings. The historical model cohorts are unpaired, "acted on" includes intentional findings, and neither finding volume nor acceptance measures missed defects. The approach role now proposes alternatives, so its historical 0.1 findings/round is insufficient grounds for removal. Astra's observed code advantage warrants targeted comparison, not replacing every Sol call.
CHECKED: Read the wrapper, role definitions, dispatch tables, ledger parsers, review synthesis and contract tests. The wrapper already supports model/effort overrides; the local Codex model catalog advertises Sol and Astra at medium/high. learn.chatgpt.com/docs/config-file/config-reference confirms reasoning configuration; code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents confirms model/effort controls and environment overrides. learn.chatgpt.com/docs/pricing distinguishes included allowance from credit billing: Astra's listed rates are 5× Sol for uncached input/output and 10× for cached input, making the cache-heavy workload material.

### 3 — Measure first, then route
WHAT: Change no defaults yet. Extend scripts/pipeline-stats.mjs, which already reads ~/.claude/projects/**.jsonl, to attribute every `codex-critic.sh` call by mode, round, model and seconds, retroactively and without new instrumentation. That explains the gap between 1,682 runs and 280 ledger rounds. Extend scripts/sol-ledger.mjs to report yield per round number. Add a replay script that re-runs saved seam inputs against candidate combinations (Sol at medium, gpt-6.1-terra at high, Sol at high as control) and scores recall of findings the ledger records as `fixed`. Defaults change in a later, separate run.
COST: A new script with tests, plus changes to two existing ones. The replay itself spends credits: 20 inputs across 3 candidates is roughly 400 credits at Sol rates, about a day and a half of current usage. Scoring recall needs a judgement of whether two findings are the same, by hand or by a model. Replay also needs the pre-revision version of each spec from git history, which exists only if each revision was committed.
GIVES UP: Any saving this run. It is the only approach that can justify terra or medium, and the only one that sizes the prize before spending on it.
CHECKED: `codex exec --json` and `codex exec resume` exist and report token counts, per a third-party test log (gist.github.com/alexfazio/359c17d84cb6a5af12bac88fa1db9770); official docs for the JSON usage field names were not found. pipeline-stats.mjs reading dispatch history is confirmed at its lines 5-15.

### 4 — Retune the role table, pipeline shape unchanged
WHAT: Every call still happens; only what each one runs on changes. Per-mode defaults in scripts/codex-critic.sh via a `case "$critic"` around lines 34-49: approach at medium; spec, code, review and refute at high; `CODEX_CRITIC_EFFORT` and `CODEX_CRITIC_MODEL` still override. On the Claude side: mechanical implementers on haiku, sweep scouts (Assignment S) on haiku via the `model` passed at skills/foureyes-investigate/SKILL.md line 84, and `effort: high` added to the four critic agent files that pin `model: opus` with no effort (agents/foureyes-spec-critic.md, -code-critic.md, -approach-critic.md, -investigate-critic.md) so a Claude-side fallback cannot inherit session xhigh. README table updated.
COST: Smallest diff of the three: the wrapper, two SKILL.md tables, four frontmatter lines, README and tests. Per-mode defaults contradict the stated rule that effort is the same on every call (codex-critic.sh lines 43-48; brainstorm SKILL.md Step 0.3), so that prose changes too.
GIVES UP: Most of the saving. Sol runs are input-dominated (~734k mostly cached input against ~4k output), so effort moves cost by tens of percent on one call in five, while a skipped call saves all of it. Wall-clock barely changes. The scout and approach-effort changes have no quality data behind them.
CHECKED: Same frontmatter verification as 1. The current Codex config reference lists effort levels low, medium, high, xhigh, max and ultra, depending on the model (search summary of developers.openai.com/codex/config-reference; page not opened). That makes the README's "`minimal` … `high` … `xhigh`" at line 245 stale. Not verified: which levels gpt-6.1-sol accepts, or the credit rates of gpt-6.1-terra and gpt-6-luna.

## Decisions

- Provenance: 1, 3, 4 — Fable (foureyes-drafter, A0). 2 — Sol (approach mode).
- Proposers' own picks: Fable → 1 (runner-up 3). Sol → 2.
- Merges: none. 2 contains a measurement leg overlapping 3, but 3 changes no defaults and adds a replay harness, so they stay separate designs.
- Re-verified (load-bearing, CHECKED thin):
  - Entry 1's adaptive round budget, against the ledger's per-round split since 2026-09-30 (light = round 1 with 0 Critical and ≤2 Important). Spec: 57 of 64 round-1s were heavy, and their round 2s still raised 39 Critical + 137 Important (168 C/I fixed; 20 of 57 round 2s had a Critical). Only 7 were light, 3 of which had a round 2 (1 Important). Code: 29 of 61 round-1s were light and only 2 of those had a round 2 at all — the existing `pass` gate already skips them. So the rule would skip about 3 spec calls in 64 and ~0 code calls: item (1) of entry 1 saves almost nothing, and the data shows spec round 2 is the most productive call in the pipeline. Items (2) and (3) are unaffected.
  - Entry 2's refute claim: confirmed. review SKILL.md Step 5 (lines 126-140) dispatches one refute call per single finding, in parallel; review-synth.mjs keys refutations by finding id (lines 55, 69).
  - Entry 4's "add effort: high to four critic agents": three of the four (spec-critic, code-critic, investigate-critic) are Codex prompt bodies marked "NOT A CLAUDE SUBAGENT"; their frontmatter effort is never read by a Claude dispatch. That item is moot; the rest of 4 stands.
- Ranking (cap 3): 2 > 3 > 1. 2 keeps discovery strength, cuts verification calls the code confirms are per-finding, pins inheriting roles, and gates any downgrade on a paired trial. 3 is the safest but changes nothing this run. 1 loses its biggest lever on the evidence above; its remaining two items are small. 4 dropped at the cap: by its own analysis it gives up most of the saving, and one of its three items is moot.
